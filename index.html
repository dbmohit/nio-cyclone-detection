Can ERA5 and CMIP6 see North Indian Ocean cyclones and their rapid intensification?

Overview

This study asks two questions. First, does the ERA5 reanalysis place North Indian Ocean cyclones correctly and capture their intensity, in particular rapid intensification (RI, a wind increase of at least 30 kt in 24 h as defined by IMD)? Second, do present-generation CMIP6 climate models produce cyclones in the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea at the right rate and strength, and do they produce RI at all?

Both questions are answered with the same two objective trackers, so that ERA5 sets a reference for what each tracker can see before it is applied to the coarser climate models.

What was done

  1. Observations. The IMD best track (IBTrACS v04, RSMC New Delhi) was used for positions, 3-hourly maximum sustained wind and central pressure of every North Indian Ocean storm since 1982.
  2. ERA5 case studies. For 28 storms (12 RI, 16 NRI) ERA5 pressure-level, sea-level pressure and 10 m wind data were downloaded for each storm's life cycle, both trackers were run, and the tracks and intensity were compared with IMD point by point.
  3. CMIP6 subset. Daily fields from 12 CMIP6 models (historical 1950–2014 and SSP5-8.5 2015–2050) were streamed from the Pangeo cloud archive and cut to 0–30°N, 60–105°E (17 GB kept out of about 3 TB).
  4. CMIP6 tracking. Both trackers were run on all 1,212 model-years, with peak 850 hPa wind, minimum pressure and day-to-day intensification computed along every track.
  5. Validation. Model storm counts, locations, seasonality and intensity were compared with IMD for the common period 1982–2014, and changes to 2050 were examined.

Main findings

    This is a preliminary analysis. Daily-mean, roughly 1° CMIP6 fields cannot resolve a cyclone core, so low counts and weak intensities are expected; the value here is the comparison between models and against ERA5.

    Study storms

    The 28 storms are the RI/NRI list used in the intensity-prediction work: 12 storms that reached RI by the IMD definition and 16 that did not. They span 2006–2023 and every intensity class from cyclonic storm (CS) to super cyclonic storm (SuCS). For each storm ERA5 was retrieved over a box around its IMD track padded by 4°, and over its life cycle padded by one day on each side.

    Trackers

    UZ (Ullrich and Zarzycki 2017, TempestExtremes). A candidate is a local minimum of sea-level pressure that is enclosed by a closed pressure rise within 5.5° and has an upper-level warm core, measured as a drop in the thickness of the upper-tropospheric layer away from the centre within 4°.

    OWZ (Tory et al. 2013). A candidate is a local maximum of 850 hPa relative vorticity that is above a floor, is enclosed by a vorticity fall within 3°, sits in a moist column (850 hPa specific humidity averaged within 2°) and is in weak vertical wind shear (850–200 hPa, averaged within 3°).

    In both trackers candidates within 6° of each other are merged, and candidates are joined in time into tracks (StitchNodes) with a maximum step of 8° and at most one missing time step. Only centres within 0–35°N, 60–105°E are kept.

    The CMIP6 thresholds are relaxed because a daily mean on a 1° grid smooths the pressure dip, the warm core and the vorticity peak of a cyclone. The same preset is used for all 12 models.

    Intensity and rapid intensification

    At each track point the minimum sea-level pressure within 2° of the centre and the maximum wind speed within 2.5° are taken. ERA5 uses 10 m wind, comparable with IMD's surface wind. CMIP6 uses 850 hPa wind, because the archived 1000 hPa level is masked below ground, which is exactly inside lows deeper than 1000 hPa (ACCESS-CM2, CESM2, MRI-ESM2-0 and KACE-1-0-G).

    ΔV24 is the change in maximum wind over 24 h. RI follows IMD: ΔV24 ≥ 30 kt. For ERA5, ΔV24 is computed from the 3-hourly series sampled at the IMD fix times; for CMIP6 it is the day-to-day change. Because ERA5 underestimates intensity, a relative threshold is also tested: ERA5 ΔV24 above the 90th percentile of all 28 storms.

    Validation measures

    Track error is the mean great-circle distance between the ERA5 track and the IMD fixes, after finding the time offset that gives the smallest error (at least 8 overlapping fixes). Intensity bias, RMSE and correlation compare ERA5 and IMD at the IMD fix times along the IMD track. Timing is the time of ERA5's largest ΔV24 minus the IMD RI onset.

    CMIP6 counts are tracks per model-year in 1982–2014, compared with the IMD count of depressions and above (≥ 17 kt) and of cyclonic storms and above (≥ 34 kt). A track counts as an ocean storm if its first point is over the sea; the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea are split at 77.5°E.

    CMIP6 models

    Zarr GB is the full global size of the five daily variables for 1950–2050 in the cloud archive; only the regional subset was saved. HadGEM3-GC31-MM and KACE-1-0-G use a 360-day calendar; HadGEM3, ACCESS-CM2 and KACE store wind on a staggered grid, which was interpolated to the pressure grid. CNRM-CM6-1-HR historical humidity ends in 1929 in the archive, so its OWZ tracks cover 2015–2050 only. One ensemble member per model.

    Limitations

    • CMIP6 data are daily means on 0.5–2.5° grids, so cyclone cores of 20–50 km and 6-hour RI bursts cannot be resolved. A daily ΔV24 also smooths the 3-hourly IMD changes.
    • ERA5 at 0.25° underestimates the peak wind of intense storms, most of all for RI storms, so ERA5 cannot be used to label RI with the IMD 30 kt rule without correction.
    • One ensemble member per model. Counts of 0.1–1 storms per year over 33 years carry large sampling error, and trends need more members.
    • The detection thresholds are one preset for all models and were not tuned per model. Tuning would change the counts but not the intensity gap.
    • CMIP6 wind is the 850 hPa wind, not the surface wind IMD reports.
    • Land or ocean genesis is judged from the first track point against the Natural Earth 50 m coastline.
    • CMIP7 is not used: its daily pressure-level output is not yet broadly published on Pangeo or ESGF.

    Next steps

    • Repeat the CMIP6 analysis with 6-hourly output (6hrPlevPt) or HighResMIP runs at 25–50 km for the three models that come closest to IMD (CNRM-CM6-1-HR, HadGEM3-GC31-MM, EC-Earth3).
    • Bias-correct ERA5 intensity against IMD (for example quantile mapping by storm intensity class) before using it to label RI.
    • Use the ERA5 timing signal: ERA5's largest ΔV24 falls within 24 h of the IMD RI onset for most RI storms, which makes ERA5 environmental fields usable as predictors for the RI prediction model.
    • Add more ensemble members for the best models to separate trend from sampling noise.
    • Merge these results into the intensity-prediction report.
